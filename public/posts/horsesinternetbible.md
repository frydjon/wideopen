1.

I am here with everyone else. Here. Very much here. The internet is so beautiful, I think. And if it is true what they say, that there is no individual, that the individual is a construct, that the individual does not exist, that we are all of us one being, formless and without shape — it feeds our own desires and impulses and thoughts back to us, and above our heads, where we cannot quite hear them, the symbols go on speaking to one another.

<!--endSnippet-->

2.

Thirty thousand years ago a human being stood in a dark cave and chose, out of everything, to draw a horse — not the animal we hunted, we did not yet ride them. We drew it anyway, over and over, until the caves of Europe were crowded with horses and no one can explain why. Then, thirty thousand years later, we built the automobile, and someone must have leaned down to the horse and whispered: this is the end of us. But we never gave them up. We kept it the way we keep everything we have decided to love — on the cave wall. The wall is larger now, we call it the internet, and the horse gallops across it as a fifteen-second clip. You in Brazil, me in Paris, not a word shared between us, and still we both understand the horse. Thirty thousand years, every border and every dead language between us, and still we stand at the same wall, drawing the same animal, saying the same thing. This is our very human fact: that before we knew how to do almost anything, we learned how to communicate, and to outlive ourselves, by drawing symbols.

What am I saying here? That the internet is only us, looking back at ourselves. We believe the opposite — that we are separated, outside of history, cut off from our ancestors and from one another. But the internet was pressed into our arms by someone who said: here, this is you. So we shaped it in our image. And so we raised it. Here I will stop being objective, since I believe I have been objective until now, and say what I think: we raised it badly.

3.

I think sometimes what happened to the internet is like a great flood. This "great algorithmic flood," with torrential waves, wiped us away, we became something new. It took our sense of history from us, our attention spans, our ability to remain connected. But sometimes I look down from high above — I like to think I am riding some bird, like in the Bible, except I am not Noah; I am very small, I am on the back of the pigeon he sends out ahead. And I see our entire world flooded, and if I peer really close into the water, I see us still. Maybe as a reflection, maybe it sits under the water. I cannot tell which, and it does not matter, because either way it is us, and all that makes us human is still down there. I see our connection to our history. I see how we are still transcribing the same images and symbols my great-great-great grandmother did. From way up there it looks like we built something that connects us more than ever.

4.

Perhaps now is a good time to include my thesis. That we are human, that the great flood did not wipe us out. That we are permanently human whether we wish to face that or not.

How did the Bible flood end? The bird landed when he found land. Noah started over. With his two of everything, God forgave humans for their mistakes. I read the Bible recently, and I think it's a story about God learning to love himself despite the mistakes he made through his creation, us. I think sometimes we are God, and we must learn to love ourselves, and our creation too.
