## Warda El Djazairia - Mouachahatte

**font:** for the latin fonts, the closest we could find was [MVB Sirenne](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/mvb-sirenne) (free) but you could also use [Apple Chancery](https://fontsarena.com/apple-chancery/) (free) which is really cool because nowadays the most common use for that font is really ironic. in this cover though, there's a true beauty accented by the colors, gradients & drop shadows. just cool to see a new use for a famous font.

**what stood out:** everything. that turquoise green, the red, the reflective cd, the yellow. "the colors real are really beautiful, and it's all tied by this consistent use of white gradients, in the symbols, borders & typography." the eyes here are really beautiful, and reminiscent of those [arabic drift racing photos](https://www.instagram.com/p/C3damtaonzY/?img_index=1) were referencing.

**about the record:** Warda was a Franco-Algerian-Lebanese singer. her dad ran a cabaret called Tam-Tam in paris during the years of french occupation of the maghreb. the cabaret served as a hub for the larger diasporic community and became notorious for drawing musicians and artists from Tunisia, Morocco & Algeria. At its height, Tam Tam was discovered to be storing weapons for the french branch of the national liberation front. Warda's father was imprisoned. Upon his release, he dedicated his life to his children's passion of singing and art. her first song recorded in the 1950s was a patriotic Algerian song. she went on to have massive success; representing a sort of Algerian national pride. [Read more about her story here.](https://www.liberation.fr/culture/1999/03/27/les-mille-et-une-voies-de-warda_268628/)

## Sweetness - Gosepl (May 1994)

**font:** closest one is [Shelley Script](https://www.myfonts.com/products/shelley-andante-script-shelley-script-982661) ($33) but you can find free alternatives. our go-to is [Pinyon Script](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Pinyon+Script)

**what stood out:** this aesthetic got somewhat played out recently, the super-over-the-top-digitized-script, typically it comes with some stretch and distortion as well. but it's cool to see it be used a while back. also, 1- pourquoi (which translates to why), 2- don't be sad. feels like a deeply poetic two song tracklist.

**about the record:** there exists very little information about this record in particular (presumably because it actually doesn't exist online.) but actually, it's nearly impossible to find anything about this. on the back cover, Patrice Lemoine is credited. as well as the fact that it was recorded in studio Garage in May of 1994. this leads us to think it was probably just a one-off cd recorded by a gospel artist.

## Umm Kulthum - Aghani Rabia Adawia

**font:** seeing as we don't speak arabic, it's really difficult to identify the font here, but instead have the free [compact disc font](https://www.dafont.com/disc.font) we found online

**what stood out:** that cream + red color palette. (#EEE5D3 & #C33432)

**about the record:** the songs featured do exist online [here.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ho4DRIxIq0A) Umm Kulthum is regarded as the 'voice of Egypt' and yet that term feels too small. she came from a small town and a deeply religious background. she grew up listening to her uncle sing the Qur'an and first started singing in choir, dressed as a boy because her father feared societal push back. unknowingly, at the time this combination of traditional Egyptian culture and gender breaking norms would go on to define her career, her legacy and the broader cultural movements of the time. "Is it possible that 50 years in Arab societies, where women appear to outsiders to be oppressed, silent and veiled, could be represented by the life and work of a woman?" -- virginia danielson, one of her biographers. for these reasons, it is nearly impossible to find a good western counterpart to her. [More about her life here.](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2020/feb/28/she-exists-out-of-time-umm-kulthum-arab-musics-eternal-star)

## Homemade Mixtape? - (2013)

**font:** you can find a bunch that have the [futuristic king of music font here](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/vs/hyraok8I6uLuAlJN7EGdrg/results?x_min=359&y_min=209&x_max=855&y_max=263)

**what stood out:** "those grafitti elements are fucking amazing." you could do some research and find some on [dreamstime](https://www.dreamstime.com/royalty-free-stock-photo-abstract-grunge-background-image2523705) (keywords: urban grunge) or train an ai model to make your own.

**about the record:** perhaps owing to our absolute lack of ability to read or understand arabic, we were unable to find anything on this record. but this might be due to the fact that this isn't an official release but a homemade mixtape or something of the sort. [This spotify playlist seems to mirror that selection](https://open.spotify.com/album/1FD6Ac7Yu8zkkp2fgc3oj2?si=YJGWSnSyR-Cbj6Yx4Qwudw) and could be made by the same people but is called best of 2014.

## Idir - Acawi & Ay Arac Ney (live recording)

**font:** that title font is impossible to find. we believe it's because it's hand drawn (this cover predates the mac being in every design studio) so maybe become a calligrapher and draw your own gothic font because its really beautiful and we need more typography like this.

**what stood out:** that font that font that font

**about the record:** this is a live recording of Idir's songs: [Acawi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z1JladPWD_g) & [Ay Arac Ney](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5_MoHtKShkM). [this is a fantastic article](https://monpetit20e.com/la-petite-histoire-du-chanteur-idir-et-des-cafes-kabyles-du-20e-arrondissement/) on Idir's life and the creation of Kabyle cafes in Paris' 20th arrondissment. also, the label that released this is still around, they focus on kabyle music, you can find [their bandcamp here](https://azwaw.bandcamp.com/).

## Meen Yeaaraf - Farid Al-Atrash

**font:** the primary font is just a basic sans serif but in the top right is the 'thrasher font' which actually predates the brand by quite a bit and was first created in marseille. [this hypebeast article](https://hypebeast.com/2016/8/thrasher-font-history-fashion-banco) does a great job telling the story behind this.

**what stood out:** the beautiful red, the trasher font in the top right. the spacing between the titles.

**about the record:** this is a recording of Farid's lp ["Meen Yeaaraf"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=esQIEWiC_j8) although we can't find anything about this particular lp. Farid wasn't just a singer he was a producer who often starred in his own movies, for which he wrote his own songs. this explains the movie frames in the lefthand side of the cover.

## Reinette L'Oranaise

**font:** again, the primary roman font is just a basic sans serif.

**what stood out:** the colors, the deep blue and that grey. but especially those graphics, "it's a much more interesting way of presenting a border. it's an implied border i guess."

**about the record:** [you can actually find this one online](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ji78NhsY3hU), although with a lot of digging. Reinette L'Oranaise was a Jewish-Algerian singer, who, at 2 contracted smallpox and went blind. her father pushes her to make music. she makes music steeped in folkloric Oranese culture, borrowing from houzi poetry & traditional instruments such as the mandolin. By 26, she's a phenom in Algier. but in 1962, as many other Jews in Algeria, she decides to leave. this is the beginning of a 20 year isolation period where she stops all public appearances and is almost, largely forgotten. it's not until 1985 that a radio host in Paris shines a new spotlight on her and pushes her back into the zeitgeist and she decides begins to start her perfomances again. [there's a documentary about her life here.](https://www.imarabe.org/fr/agenda/cinema/projection-reinette-oranaise-port-amours-jacqueline-gozland)
